Planning For A Growing Coffee Business

Less than a year into business, a coffee company operates three shops across Singapore, supported by one central inventory and distribution hub.

Its 80 products range from freshly prepared coffee, tea and chocolate drinks to pastries, packaged coffee and merchandise. The hub purchases and holds ingredients, cups and ready-to-sell goods, then supplies the shops for daily service.

With six months of sales already recorded, the team wants to anticipate what customers will require over the next week and the next full month. Turning those records into forecasts can help the business prepare enough stock while limiting excess.

This release focuses on demand forecasting. Inventory and distribution decisions are future extensions once starting stock, lead times, supplier constraints and costs are available.

1. Start with what the shops sell

The three shops offer 80 products across nine categories, from freshly made drinks to pastries, packaged goods and merchandise. Before the central hub can plan its stock, we need to distinguish what arrives ready to sell from what the team prepares at the counter. There are 39 ready-to-sell products and 41 prepared drinks. A pastry sale uses one stocked pastry; a drink sale uses ingredients and packaging. That difference determines what the hub needs to buy.

2. Connect each product to what the hub stocks

The categories give us the broad picture; the next step is to identify the stock behind each individual product. A bill of materials (BOM) lists the quantities needed for one sale. For example, a Latte uses beans, milk, a cup and a lid, while a ready-to-sell item maps directly to the product itself.

The tables below use the project’s defined recipes. Sm, Rg and Lg mean Small, Regular and Large. Shared ingredients appear in more than one recipe because the hub must supply them for all the drinks that use them.
